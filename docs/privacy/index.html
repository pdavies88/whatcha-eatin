Whatcha Eatin'

Privacy Policy

Effective October 23, 2026

The short version: Whatcha Eatin' has no accounts, no servers, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. I, the developer, never receive your photos, meals or API keys. Your data stays on your phone. There are two exceptions. A photo or meal description you choose to analyze goes straight from your phone to the AI provider you picked. A barcode you scan is looked up in the free Open Food Facts database.

Who this policy covers

Whatcha Eatin' is an iPhone and Android app made by Preston Davies, an independent developer. This policy explains what the app does with your information. If you have questions, email preston5@protonmail.com.

What stays on your phone

The app stores these things only on your device, inside the app's private storage:

  • Your AI provider API keys. On iPhone, these are stored in the iOS Keychain, are only readable while your phone is unlocked, and are never synced to iCloud Keychain. On Android, they are encrypted with a key held in the Android Keystore.
  • Your meals. For each meal you save, the app keeps the nutrition estimate, the time you saved it, and which AI provider and model produced it. For meals you typed in, it also keeps what you typed.
  • Your meal photos. The app keeps a resized copy of each saved meal's photo.
  • Your settings. The app remembers which AI provider and model you selected.
  • Your profile, if you add one. Your age, sex, height, weight, activity level and goal are used on your phone to estimate your daily calorie and macro targets. They are never sent anywhere, including to AI providers.

The app automatically deletes meals and their photos after seven days. Each day, meals from eight days ago are removed. You can also delete any meal yourself.

If you delete the app, your phone deletes its meals, photos and settings. On iPhone, iOS may keep Keychain items after an app is deleted, so to make sure your API keys are removed, delete them in the app's Settings before you delete the app.

If you back up your phone, the backup can include the app's saved meals and photos. That means iCloud or a computer on iPhone, and Google's Android backup on Android. API keys are not restored to a different phone: on iPhone they are "this device only" Keychain items, and on Android they are excluded from backups.

What leaves your phone

Data leaves your phone only when you choose to analyze a photo or a typed meal. At that point, the app sends these things directly from your phone to the AI provider you selected in Settings:

  • a resized copy of the photo, if you took one,
  • the meal description or optional notes you typed, and
  • instructions asking the AI to estimate calories and macros.

The request uses your own API key, so it goes to your own account with that provider. The provider's privacy policy and terms then apply to that data, including how long they keep it and whether they use it to improve their products. I have no access to your provider account or to what you send.

The supported providers and their privacy policies are:

  • Google Gemini: Google Privacy Policy and Gemini API Additional Terms
  • OpenAI: OpenAI Privacy Policy
  • Anthropic: Anthropic Privacy Policy
  • xAI: xAI Privacy Policy
  • OpenRouter: OpenRouter Privacy Policy

A note on Gemini's free tier: Google's terms let Google use content sent through free, unpaid Gemini API keys to improve its products. If you don't want that, turn on billing for your Google project or choose a different provider.

The app also sends your API key to the provider when you save it or load the provider's model list, so the provider can confirm the key works.

Barcode lookups

When you scan or type a barcode, the app sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free, open database of packaged foods, to look up the product's name, nutrition facts and photo. No API key, account, photo or personal details are sent with it. If you save the product, the app downloads its photo from Open Food Facts and stores it on your phone with the meal. Open Food Facts' privacy policy covers its servers.

Product data comes from Open Food Facts under the Open Database License, and product photos are licensed CC BY-SA. The app credits Open Food Facts wherever it shows them.

What the app does not do

  • It does not collect any data for the developer.
  • It does not use analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking tools.
  • It does not track you across other apps or websites.
  • It does not sell or share your data.
  • It does not require an account or ask for your name, email or location.

Camera access

The app asks for camera access so you can photograph your food and scan barcodes. It does not ask for access to your photo library. You can change this permission at any time in your phone's Settings app.

Links to other websites

The app links to provider websites, such as Google AI Studio, so you can create an API key. Those sites open in an in-app browser and have their own privacy policies.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13, and it does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Health information

Calorie and macro numbers from photos and typed meals are AI estimates. They can be wrong by 20% or more. Barcode results come from package labels entered by Open Food Facts contributors and can contain mistakes. Daily targets are general estimates from a standard formula. None of this is medical advice, and the app is not a medical device.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I will update this page and its effective date. Major changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
